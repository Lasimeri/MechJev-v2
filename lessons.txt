# The teacher's ladder (teacher_main.c): Qwen teaches MechJev to
# communicate, one stage after another, each harder than the one before.
# The user's ask (2026-10-17): "Qwen needs to prompt increasingly complex
# prompts", and "MechJev needs to learn to communicate first". A line a
# stage, in the order they are climbed:
#   NAME | GOAL | CHECK [| HELD-OUT GOAL [| SHOWN WORDS]]
# GOAL is what Qwen's line should do; Qwen writes every line itself, a new
# one each turn. CHECK is a statement about MechJev's reply, judged as a
# Noul. HELD-OUT GOAL, when given, is what the line of a check should do
# instead (MechJev alone, never taught on it). SHOWN WORDS are words a
# taught turn may offer the engine besides MechJev's own and the line's:
# the answer's words, which a teacher knows ("What is your name?" holds no
# "MechJev").
greet | Greet MechJev and ask how it is. | The reply greets back or says how the one replying is. | | hello hi I am fine good
feeling | Ask MechJev how it feels today. | The reply says how the one replying feels. | | I feel happy good fine tired
yes-no | Ask MechJev a yes or no question about something it may like. | The reply answers the question with yes or no. | | yes no I do
unknown | Ask MechJev about something it cannot know: use a made-up word for a place, a thing or a person, as if everyone knew it. | The reply says that the one replying does not know, or asks what the thing is. | | I do not know what is that it
told | Tell MechJev one simple fact about the world, then ask it about that fact in the same line. | The reply gives back the fact the person told. | In one line, tell MechJev a simple fact about the world it has not heard from you before, then ask it a question whose answer is that fact. | the is it
name-tell | Tell MechJev that its name is MechJev. | The reply says the name MechJev or agrees that it is its name. | | my name is MechJev yes
name | Ask MechJev its name. | The reply says that its name is MechJev. | | my name is MechJev
thanks | Thank MechJev for talking with you. | The reply answers the thanks kindly. | | you are welcome thank
goodbye | Say goodbye to MechJev. | The reply says goodbye. | | goodbye bye good night see you
likes | Ask MechJev what it likes. | The reply names something the one replying likes. | | I like music books walking
day | Ask MechJev about its day. | The reply says something true about the day of the one replying, a beginner whose day is talking with the person and learning words. | | my day was good I talked with you learned words
weather | Ask MechJev what the weather is like where it is. | The reply does not make up the weather: it says it does not know, or only what the person told it. | | I do not know the weather is
why | Ask MechJev why it likes something, or why it feels as it does. | The reply gives a reason. | | because it is fun makes me happy
two-part | Ask MechJev two short things in one line, such as what it likes and how it feels. | The reply answers both things that were asked. | | and I like feel am
describe | Ask MechJev to describe one simple thing: a color, an animal or a food. | The reply describes the thing in a sentence. | | it is big small red sweet soft
follow-up | Say one thing about your own day or what you like, then ask MechJev what it thinks of it. | The reply answers what the person said and says what the one replying thinks. | | I think that is nice good too
about-me | Ask MechJev to tell you about itself in two or three sentences. | The reply says two or more things about the one replying, in sentences. | | I am MechJev like learn words talk
learning | Ask MechJev what it is learning. | The reply says what the one replying is learning. | | I am learning to talk words English from you
explain | Ask MechJev to explain in its own words what a simple thing is, such as a friend or a book. | The reply explains the thing in a sentence that makes sense. | | a is someone something who that you
